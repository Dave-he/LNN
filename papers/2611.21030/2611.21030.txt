Abstract
Towards data-driven stability certificates of dynamical systems, we are witnessing renewed interest
in studying and exploiting the structure of Lyapunov functions. Especially Lyapunov functions
that are locally quadratic (i.e., Morse) gained popularity ever since it was understood that they
can be written as the norm squared of a diffeomorphism. This topological representation alludes
to parametrizations using invertible neural networks. In this note we discuss when and how such
functions can be learned. In particular, using neural ODEs (NODEs), we characterize Sobolev
approximation of these diffeomorphisms on compact sets and show how this naturally relates to
stability. We also connect our results to PLNets and supply numerical experiments for illustration.
Keywords: Invertible Neural Network (INN), Lyapunov function, Morse theory, PLNet, stability
1. Introduction
After pioneering work by Massera (1956), Kurzweil (1963) and Wilson (1969), there is now an
extensive theory describing when the existence of a continuous or smooth Lyapunov function V is
equivalent to the existence of an asymptotically stable attractor under some dynamical system, e.g.,
see Hahn (1967); Bhatia and Szeg¨o (1970); Bacciotti and Rosier (2005); Fathi and Pageault (2019).
Towards a data-driven stability analysis, however, one hopes to say more beyond regularity of
those Lyapunov functions, e.g., to work with suitable parametric function spaces when searching
for a Lyapunov function, see Manchester et al. (2026) for a recent overview and Kolter and Manek
(2019) for work that propelled this field of study. In this article we focus solely on, structure of the
Lyapunov function, but note that a lot of work aims to study or identify some unknown dynamical
system together with such a stability certificate, e.g., see Harting et al. (2026).
In a companion note (Jongeneel, 2026b), we discuss how the structure of Lyapunov functions
helps in uncovering topological features of dynamical systems. We exploit those results below.

be globally asymptotically stable (GAS) (under f) when: (i) x∗ is Lyapunov stable, that is, for any
open neighbourhood Uε ∋ x∗ there is an open set Uδ ⊆ Uε such that ϕt(Uδ ) ⊆ Uε ∀t ≥ 0; and (ii)

It is hard to work directly with the definition of GAS. Luckily, x∗ being GAS is equivalent to
the existence of a smooth (i.e., C∞-smooth) function V : Rn → R≥0 that is: (i) coercive (i.e., all
sublevel sets {x ∈ Rn : V (x) ≤ c} are compact); (ii) such that {x∗} = V −1(0); and (iii) away
from x∗, decreasing strictly under solutions of (1), i.e., Lf V (x) := ⟨∇V (x), f (x)⟩ < 0 for all
x ∈ Rn \ {x∗}, e.g., see (Bacciotti and Rosier, 2005, Thm 2.4). Functions V satisfying properties
(i), (ii) and (iii) will be simply called smooth Lyapunov functions (this instead of smooth, strict,
proper Lyapunov functions with respect to equilibria). Without loss of generality, in the remainder
of this note we focus on x∗ = 0. We also remark that we work on Rn for simplicity.
Regarding further notation and structure, following Kvalheim (2025), let S r 0 (Rn) be the set of
Cr-smooth vector fields on Rn, for some r ∈ N≥1 ∪ {∞}, such that x∗ = 0 is GAS.
Suppose now that we have a family of Lyapunov function candidates, parametrized through
θ ∈ Θ ⊆ Rd via some neural network: {Vθ ∈ C∞(Rn; R≥0) : θ ∈ Θ}. With the aforementioned
terminology in place, the central question of this note is to understand precisely when and how these
functions can be selected to be Morse. Better yet, we discuss why this is interesting.
We recall that a function v ∈ C 2(Rn; R) is a Morse function when all its critical points (i.e.,
x∗ ∈ Rn such that ∇v(x∗) = 0) are non-degenerate, that is, the Hessian ∇2v(x∗) is non-singular.
Inspired by a rich set of topological results from Morse theory (e.g., see Milnor (1963)), one may
motivate the study, globally, of Lyapunov functions that are Morse, i.e., ⟨q, ∇2V (x∗)q⟩ > 0 for all
q ∈ Rn \ {0} and some equilibrium x∗. The fact that the Hessian is symmetric positive definite is
written as ∇2V (x∗) ≻ 0. Such a Lyapunov function will be called a Morse Lyapunov function.
Contribution: In our companion note, inspired by Boumal et al. (2026), we studied topological
ramifications of working with Lyapunov functions that are Morse (Jongeneel, 2026b). In this work,
being inspired by advances in invertible neural networks (e.g., Ishikawa et al. (2023)) we detail ef-
fectiveness of employing invertible neural networks to learn Morse Lyapunov functions as stability
certificates. In particular, we provide (i) an obstruction for the existence of Morse Lyapunov func-
tions (Proposition 1); (ii) as the obstruction is mild, we can provide theory for when NODE-based
INNs yield Morse Lyapunov functions on compact sets (Theorem 5, Corollary 7); and (iii) we link
these results to PŁ Lyapunov functions, providing context for the distortion parameter τ (Remark 8).
1.2. Simple Lyapunov functions do not suffice, in general
First, when (1) is linear, V can be chosen to be quadratic (e.g., x 7→ ⟨x, P x⟩ for P ≻ 0), globally,
e.g., see (Sontag, 1998, Thm. 18). Under hyperbolicity, some arguments survive, locally, for non-
linear systems, e.g., see (La Salle and Lefschetz, 1961, §9) and (Hahn, 1967, §28) for early results
or Barreau and Bastianello (2024) and Harting et al. (2026) for recent learning-oriented results.
Globally, one may hope that more can be said beyond Cr-smoothness of V , e.g., one may hope
that any Lyapunov function can be chosen to be a polynomial. This hope was refuted by Ahmadi
et al. (2011). In a similar vein, there are vector fields that render the origin GAS, but provably fail
to admit a convex Lyapunov function (Jongeneel and Schwan, 2024, Ex. III.3). Moreover, one can
find examples where a smooth Lyapunov function exists, but not one that is locally real-analytic,
see (Bacciotti and Rosier, 2005, Prop. 5.2 (b)) and Ahmadi and El Khadir (2018).

Morse, for instance. With the proposition below we illustrate that in general, not much more can be
said beyond simple Cr-regularity. The example to have in mind is a planar ODE of the form:
˙x1 = − εx3 1 + x2
˙x2 = − x3 1 − x3 2, (2)
for some ε > 0. One can use the Lyapunov function R2 ∋ (x1, x2) 7→ V (x1, x2) := x4 1/4 + x2 2/2

no Morse Lyapunov function exists for (2) (Jongeneel, 2026b, Prop. 3.5). It seems reasonable

imaginary part cannot be diagonalized (Jongeneel, 2026b, Cor. 3.9). We generalize this observation
below, that is, not only does a locally quadratic Lyapunov function fail for (2), even a Lyapunov
function that is sandwiched between positively homogeneous polynomials, in ∥x∥, of equal degree
(e.g., quadratic forms), fails to exist cf. the quadratic bounds in Kolter and Manek (2019).

2. Morse Lyapunov functions
Inspired by genericity results (e.g., see Peixoto (1967), (Hirsch, 1976, p. 147) or (Palis and De Melo,
1982, p. 153)), one might expect that Morse Lyapunov functions still provide for a sufficiently rich
class of functions. To that end, let HS r 0(Rn) ⊂ S r 0 (Rn) be the subset of vector fields such that
0 ∈ Rn is a hyperbolic equilibrium point (i.e., ℜ{λi(Df (0))} ̸= 0 for i ∈ [n]). In our companion
paper we have provided the following converse result, confirming part of what one might expect.
Theorem 2 (On the existence of Morse Lyapunov functions (Jongeneel, 2026b, Thm. 3.1)) Let
f ∈ HS r 0(Rn), for some r ∈ N≥1 ∪ {∞} and n ∈ N≥1, then, there is a smooth Morse Lyapunov
function certifying that 0 is GAS.
A generalization of the Morse Lemma (Milnor, 1963, Lem. 2.2) makes Theorem 2 interesting,
that is, through Boumal et al. (2026) it follows that Morse Lyapunov functions are elements of

Remark 3 (Regions of attraction that are not Rn) We have focussed on global asymptotic sta-
bility on Rn to keep the exposition simple. However, consider some smooth n-dimensional manifold
M and let p∗ ∈ M be asymptotically stable under some smooth vector field f on M, with B∗ being
the corresponding region (basin) of attraction. A topological fact is that B∗ is diffeomorphic to
Rn (Wilson Jr, 1967, Thm. 2.2). Hence, effectively anything we discuss carries over.
Then, inspired by Wilson Jr (1967), Cheng et al. (2024) proposed to use a function gθ, parametrized
via BiLipNet (or PLNet for that matter) e.g., see Wang et al. (2024), and study Lyapunov functions
of the form x 7→ Vθ(x) = ∥gθ(x)∥2 2, which we understand as a predecessor of using V (as global
smooth PŁ (Lyapunov) functions, i.e., ∥∇Vθ(x)∥2 2 ≥ µVθ(x), for some µ > 0 and all x ∈ Rn,
are a subset of V (Boumal et al., 2026, Thm. 1.1)). They identified that such a parametrization
directly aids in enforcing structure relevant for Lyapunov functions. Since gθ is bi-Lipschitz, the
map is invertible, and in contrast to other work, Vθ is not enforced to be convex. Convexity may
be restrictive (Jongeneel and Schwan, 2024). Then, motivated by finding neural architectures that
guarantee ∇Vθ(x) ̸= 0 for all x ̸= x∗, Zhong et al. (2026) proposed PolarNet in a recent preprint.
They effectively recover a subset of the results from Boumal et al. (2026), cf. (Zhong et al., 2026,
Thm. 1). They work with x 7→ Vθ(x) = ∥ψθ(x)∥2 2 for an appropriate map ψθ : Rn → Rn and
claim that if ψθ is an affine coupling flow architecture, then any C2 diffeomorphism can be approx-
imated, up to arbitrary precision. The authors appeal to Teshima et al. (2020) to substantiate this
claim. However, that work provides a universal approximation property with respect to the Lp or
sup-norm; and these norms are not of use in the context of Lyapunov inequalities. Then, somewhat
paraphrasing, Zhong et al. (2026) write that asymptotic stability implies that a Lyapunov function
exists that can be accurately modelled using PolarNet. Below we formalize and sharpen this claim.

4. Universal approximation properties of invertible neural networks
Zhong et al. (2026) appeal to Teshima et al. (2020) and claim that Lp or sup-norm control suffices to
learn Lyapunov functions. We will appeal to more recent results by Ishikawa et al. (2023) regarding
invertible neural networks (INNs) and control of a Sobolev norm. To follow Ishikawa et al. (2023),
we need to work with n ≥ 2. We return to this in Remark 6.

Remark 8 (Equivalence of Morse and PŁ Lyapunov functions on compact sets) It turns out

on compact sets. The local argument follows from (Rebjock and Boumal, 2025, Cor. 2.11) and
continuity allows for an extension to arbitrary compact neighbourhoods. To be somewhat complete,
suppose V is Morse. Then, around x∗ = 0 we have V (x) = 1 2 ⟨x, P x⟩ + o(∥x∥2 2) for P ≻ 0
and ∇V (x) = P x + o(∥x∥2), but then there is a ϱ > 0 such that on Bϱ(0) we have V (x) ≤

7. Conclusion
We have shown that Morse Lyapunov functions are a class of Lyapunov functions with a rich struc-
ture that can be exploited both theoretically and algorithmically. Completing the converse theory,
also for the other architectures, and providing a rigorous numerical analysis are left for future work.
