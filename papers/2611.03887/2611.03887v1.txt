The Information Limits of Neural Controlled Differential Equations
Under Sparse Observations
Rostislav Gusev 1 Evgenia Romanenkova 1 Alexey Zaytsev 1
Abstract
Neural controlled differential equations (Neural
CDEs) evolve hidden states along continuous
paths constructed by interpolating discrete ob-
servations. Sparse measurements leave the in-
put between observation times undetermined, and
admissible continuous paths consistent with the
same data can produce different terminal states.
We characterize this ambiguity for planar drivers
with bounded speed. The set of compatible ter-
minal states admits a leading-order zonotopic ap-
proximation determined by feasible signed areas
and transported Lie brackets. Its projection onto
a scalar prediction yields a computable, target-
specific ambiguity certificate. We also study the
number of measurements needed to reduce the
remaining uncertainty. For nondegenerate pla-
nar CDEs, the optimal worst-case radius over de-
terministic adaptive sampling policies scales as
N −1 when speed is bounded and N −2 when both
speed and acceleration are bounded, where N is
the number of observation intervals. Synthetic ex-
periments assess the certificate using admissible
continuous completions and examine the role of
path regularity.
1. Introduction
Neural controlled differential equations (Neural CDEs) pro-
cess irregularly sampled time series by evolving hidden
states along continuous control paths constructed from dis-
crete observations (Kidger et al., 2020; Morrill et al., 2022).
Interpolation specifies the control between sampled times.
When the observations come from an underlying contin-
uous signal, its evolution between measurements remains
unknown. Different continuous paths can agree with every
recorded value and still drive the same CDE to different
terminal states.

These differences arise from path geometry that point obser-
vations do not capture. The observed endpoints determine
the increments of a planar control, while the signed areas
traced between them remain undetermined. In a CDE with
noncommuting vector fields, these areas influence the hid-
den state, and their effects propagate through the subsequent
dynamics. The resulting variation in the terminal response
depends on the geometry permitted by the observations and
the sensitivity of the CDE to that geometry.
The size of this ambiguity matters when a Neural CDE is
used to model a continuously driven system. Observation
schedules with the same number of measurements can con-
strain a prediction to different degrees, depending on where
the missing path information affects the dynamics. An in-
terpolated control provides a prediction, while the range
of responses across compatible continuous paths describes
how much of that prediction is determined by the avail-
able measurements. Quantifying this range can help assess
the adequacy of a sampling schedule and identify intervals
where additional observations would be informative.
Previous analyses of Neural CDEs have examined interpola-
tion schemes and sampling-dependent approximation and
generalization (Morrill et al., 2022; Bleistein & Guilloux,
2024). Higher-order signatures and Lie brackets have also
been used to understand and approximate CDE dynamics
(Walker et al., 2024). We study how the information missing
from a discrete observation record affects the responses of
a fixed CDE. The admissible inputs are continuous paths
satisfying the observed values and prescribed regularity con-
straints. Their terminal responses form the identified set
associated with the observation record.
Our main results are summarized below.
• Geometry of partial identification. We character-
ize the leading terminal-state identified set for planar
CDEs with bounded-speed inputs. Feasible signed ar-
eas and transported Lie brackets determine a zonotopic
approximation with a uniform Hausdorff error bound.
• Target-specific ambiguity certificate. We derive a
computable certificate for scalar predictions that com-
bines the unresolved path geometry on each observa-

2. Related Work
Neural CDEs and sparse observations. Kidger et al.
(2020) introduced Neural CDEs for irregularly sampled
and partially observed time series, using continuous con-
trol paths constructed from discrete measurements. The
choice of interpolation affects the information available to
the model, particularly in online prediction (Morrill et al.,
2022). More recent work develops injective embeddings of
irregular data without reconstructing the observed variables
(Walker et al., 2026). Theoretical analyses by Bleistein
& Guilloux (2024) establish sampling-dependent general-
ization bounds and characterize sampling-induced approx-
imation bias using continuity properties of CDE solutions.
These results describe how sampling and continuous repre-
sentations affect Neural CDE predictions.
Path geometry and uncertain dynamics. Higher-order
path information influences controlled dynamics through
iterated integrals and Lie brackets. Log-NCDEs exploit this
structure to approximate CDE solutions using higher-order
log-signatures (Walker et al., 2024). In stochastic settings,
Malham & Wiese (2014) study the conditional distribution
of Levy area for a Brownian path with prescribed endpoints.
Inverse problems for rough differential equations investi-
gate recovery of the driving signal from observed responses
(Bailleul & Diehl, 2015; Morrish et al., 2026). Reachability
analysis studies the sets of states generated by admissible
inputs (Althoff et al., 2021). Discrete observations of the
driver impose a different constraint: the compatible input
paths must agree at the measured times, and their unresolved
geometry determines the range of possible CDE responses.
Optimal recovery and information limits. Optimal re-
covery theory establishes worst-case limits on reconstruct-
ing functions from finitely many observations (Micchelli
et al., 1976). Information-based complexity provides a
general framework for studying the information required
to approximate a functional (Traub et al., 1988). Related
questions arise for stochastic differential equations. M¨uller-
Gronbach (2004) derive minimal approximation errors from
sequential observations of the driving Brownian motion and
construct asymptotically optimal adaptive methods. The
information radius considered here measures the worst-
case variation of a CDE output over observation-compatible
drivers satisfying deterministic regularity constraints. Its
dependence on transported Lie-bracket sensitivity connects
these information limits to the dynamics and the prediction
target.
Adaptive computation and observation design. Adap-
tive numerical methods use estimates of local error to al-
locate computational effort. Moon et al. (2003) establish
convergence and complexity results for adaptive ODE ap-
proximation, while Bayer et al. (2023) develop an adaptive
RDE solver based on local error contributions to the global
solution error. Active sensing concerns the acquisition of
additional measurements. Qin et al. (2023) develop a Neural-
CDE-based sensing policy that selects observation times and
measured variables under prediction-risk and acquisition-
cost objectives. In the present setting, measurement loca-
tions are evaluated through the ambiguity remaining among
admissible continuous drivers, linking observation design
to the radius of information.

5. Minimax Limits of Observation
For a fixed set of observations, the identified set describes
the remaining uncertainty in the CDE response. We study
how this uncertainty decreases as more measurements be-
come available. The minimax radius RN , defined in (3),
gives the smallest worst-case radius attainable by a deter-
ministic adaptive policy. Throughout this section, the driver
is planar, the endpoints X0 and XT are observed without
cost, and the policy may request at most N − 1 additional
values.

6. Experiments
We train Neural CDEs on densely observed planar trajecto-
ries and evaluate them using progressively sparser observa-
tions, keeping the model parameters fixed. The experiments
include two path-dependent regression targets, signed area
and the output of a nonlinear controlled system, together
with an endpoint-dependent negative control. All reported
diameters and output spreads are normalized by the training-
set standard deviation of the corresponding target. The
experimental procedures and additional results are provided
in Appendices H and I.
6.1. Certificate accuracy for admissible completions
We evaluate the certificate by comparing its predicted di-
ameter 2R2(S) with the output variation of two admissi-
ble continuous paths sharing the same observations. On
each observation interval, circular-arc completions attain
the maximal positive and negative signed areas permitted
by the speed bound. The first path chooses the sign of each
area according to the corresponding coefficient ci, and the
second uses the opposite signs. Both paths agree at every
observation time and satisfy the prescribed speed constraint.
Their terminal output difference provides a feasible lower
bound on the identified-set diameter.
Across 96 evaluations of the path-dependent targets, the
predicted diameter and the feasible output spread have a
Spearman correlation of 0.9987. The median ratio of fea-
sible to predicted diameter is 0.981. As the number of
observations increases from five to 33, the median relative
discrepancy decreases from 5.49% to 1.69% (Figure 2, left).
The feasible spread decreases with a fitted log–log slope
of 1.01 against the maximum observation gap, while the
absolute discrepancy decreases with a slope of 1.55. For the
endpoint-dependent negative control, the median certificate
diameter is only 0.07% of the corresponding scale for path-
dependent targets.
6.2. Effect of path regularity
We compare the bounded-speed certificate with the
acceleration-aware leading outer approximation on smooth
input paths satisfying both velocity and acceleration con-
straints. Across 72 evaluations of path-dependent targets,
the acceleration-aware approximation has a median width
equal to 7.11% of the bounded-speed certificate. The fitted
sampling exponents are 1.04 and 2.26, respectively (Fig-
ure 2, right).
To assess the acceleration-aware approximation, we opti-
mize over globally C 1 admissible completions whose ve-
locities agree at observation times. The resulting feasible
output spread has a Spearman correlation of 0.975 with
the outer approximation, but its median magnitude is only
9.11% of that approximation.
The outer approximation combines bounds obtained sepa-
rately on individual observation intervals. Globally C 1 paths
must also satisfy velocity compatibility across neighboring
intervals, and the numerical search explores a restricted
family of admissible completions. The observed difference
therefore does not determine how much conservatism arises
from each source. The fitted exponents describe the finite-
resolution certificates; the minimax results in Section 5
concern worst-case ambiguity over adaptive observation
policies.
6.3. Certificate-guided observation design
We evaluate sequential measurement selection starting from
five uniformly or irregularly spaced observations. Each pol-
icy acquires six additional measurements, selecting the mid-
point of one observation interval at each step. The trained
CDE remains fixed, and its ambiguity certificate is recom-
puted after every acquisition.
We compare the rule that selects the largest local contribu-
tion A⋆ i |ci| with a closed-form midpoint-reduction score,
longest-gap selection, geometry-only and sensitivity-only
scores, and random selection. A one-step look-ahead oracle
evaluates the underlying signal at every candidate midpoint
and selects the measurement producing the smallest recom-
puted certificate radius.
We measure acquisition performance by the area under
the certificate-radius curve, normalized by its initial value.
Lower values indicate faster reduction of the certificate over
the six acquisitions.
Across 24 initial observation records, the median normalized
area is 3.633 for both largest-local-ambiguity and midpoint-
reduction selection. The one-step oracle achieves 3.626,
compared with 3.823 for longest-gap selection and 4.544
for random selection.
The two certificate-guided rules choose different initial in-
tervals in four of the 24 cases. The midpoint-reduction score
produces a smaller recomputed radius in one of those four
cases. On this benchmark, selecting the largest local ambi-
guity achieves nearly the same certificate reduction as the
one-step oracle, with no additional improvement from the
midpoint-reduction score.

7. Discussion and Limitations
The identified set characterizes the terminal responses of
a fixed CDE across continuous paths compatible with the
observations and prescribed regularity bounds. When the
driver represents a physical signal, its width quantifies the
effect of unobserved evolution between measurements. If
the continuous path serves only as a computational represen-
tation of discrete data, the certificate instead approximates
the variation in model outputs across the prescribed class of
alternative continuous reconstructions. Predictive accuracy
also depends on the learned dynamics and the relationship
between the CDE output and the target variable.
The admissible path class determines the range of pos-
sible responses. A bounded-speed assumption gives an
exact feasible signed-area interval on each observation
gap. Bounding acceleration further restricts this range and
requires velocities to agree across neighboring intervals.
The acceleration-aware certificate consequently provides an
outer approximation to the leading ambiguity. Its practi-
cal interpretation requires regularity bounds that reflect the
underlying signal, since bounds that exclude the true path
invalidate the corresponding identification guarantee.
The present geometric characterization applies to planar
drivers. The minimax rates additionally require nonvanish-
ing transported Lie-bracket sensitivity and concern determin-
istic adaptive observation policies. For higher-dimensional
inputs, the unresolved second-level geometry contains cou-
pled area components whose joint feasibility must be charac-
terized. Establishing a corresponding identified-set approxi-
mation would extend the certificate beyond planar controls.
The certificate-guided acquisition rule also raises the ques-
tion of how closely its sequential performance approaches
the optimal adaptive information radius.
Our experiments evaluate the geometric certificate and se-
quential measurement selection on synthetic planar signals.
Assessing their use for physical sensing requires densely
recorded continuous processes, where additional measure-
ments can be acquired and their effect on predictive perfor-
mance evaluated.
8. Conclusion
Discrete observations of a continuous driver can leave the
terminal response of a fixed CDE undetermined. For planar
bounded-speed inputs, we characterize the leading geometry
of the response identified set through feasible signed areas
and transported Lie brackets, and derive a computable cer-
tificate for scalar predictions. The certificate quantifies how
the unobserved geometry on each observation interval con-
tributes to uncertainty in a given output. For nondegenerate
planar CDEs, the minimax residual radius under determin-
istic adaptive sampling scales as N −1 with bounded speed
and as N −2 when acceleration is also bounded. The result-
ing characterization connects the information missing from
a given observation record with the rate at which further
measurements can reduce the ambiguity.
