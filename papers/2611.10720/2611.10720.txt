Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly adopted as end-to-end driving
planners, yet they suffer from cross-city domain shift. Existing methods treat this
shift as a single quantity addressed by uniform alignment or single-site editing. We
challenge this view on two counts:(i) domain shift in VLM trajectory planners
is inherently structured across modality and layer, and (ii) a locate-then-repair
recipe matched to this structure outperforms every uniform-objective baseline.
We analyse domain shift via activation patching and uncover two regularities.
(F1) Modality axis.Image and text tokens exhibit distinct shift patterns in each
VLM.(F2) Layer axis.The layer-wise shift partition is architecture-dependent and
dataset-stable. These findings recast domain shift as atwo-dimensional tensor
indexed bymodality(image vs. text tokens) andlayer, motivating a simple principle:
locate where shift occurs, and repair only there. We instantiate this principle in
MoLaRx(Modality–LayerRepair), a locate-then-repair framework. The Causal
Importance Score (CIS) estimates the domain shift as a modality–layer tensor and
partitions the layers into functional zones. CIS-Guided Selective Repair (CISR)
then applies a matched operator (Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) / low-rank
adaptation (LoRA) / freezing) to repair each zone, with the composition adapting
automatically to each architecture. We evaluateMoLaRxon three architecturally
distinct VLMs (LFM2-VL-1.6B, Qwen2-VL-2B, and PaliGemma2-3B) across
two cross-city benchmarks (nuScenes and Argoverse 2). MoLaRx attains the
lowest cross-city trajectory-error gap among adaptation methods in all six main-
table settings, while using 50–60% fewer trainable parameters and 8.4–10.9 ms
lower inference latency. In contrast, uniform MMD-only adaptation collapses
in all six settings, highlighting the importance of structure-guided repair. Code:
https://github.com/ZhihongCui/MoLaRx.

(from 0.15 m to 1.13 m). Since collecting and annotating data for every new city is impractical,
mitigating this gap is critical for safe and scalable deployment.
Two paradigms in the broader literature shape how such cross-domain gaps are tackled in deep
models. The first isuniform domain adaptation, a broad class of methods that align source and
target distributions, including adversarial training [Ganin et al., 2016], distribution matching [Sun
et al., 2016, Gretton et al., 2012], image translation [Zhu et al., 2017, Yang and Soatto, 2020], and
parameter-efficient adaptation [Hu et al., 2022]. Despite their differences, these methods apply one
common adaptation strategy across layers. The second islocate-then-edit, originally developed
for single-fact knowledge editing in language models [Meng et al., 2022a] and later extended to
multimodal LLMs [Cheng et al., 2023]. ROME [Meng et al., 2022a] uses causal tracing to identify
an MLP site and applies a rank-one weight update there; MMEdit [Cheng et al., 2023] benchmarks
model editing in multimodal settings. Across both strategies, the same assumption is encoded:
domain shift is treated as asingle quantitythat uniform alignment or single-site editing can target.
We argue that this scalar assumption is fundamentally misaligned with structured domain shift, as it
enforces a single objective across heterogeneous components, inevitably disrupting domain-invariant
computation.We therefore ask two questions: (Q1) what is the structural form of cross-city domain
shift in VLM trajectory planners? and (Q2) given the answer, what repair recipe outperforms
uniform alignment?We answer empirically: cross-city domain shift is inherently structured along
modality and layer axes(Q1), and a locate-then-repair recipe matched to this structure dominates
every uniform-objective baseline(Q2).
To establish this empirically, we need a causal diagnostic, but standard tools fall short. Correlational
probes such as domain probing accuracy (DPA) [Alain and Bengio, 2016, Belinkov, 2022, Tas and
Wagner, 2024] saturate above95% at every layer of each VLM (Figure 1 (b)), uninformative about
causal contribution; attention weights [Jain and Wallace, 2019] are similarly correlational; gradient
attribution [Sundararajan et al., 2017] conflates causal effect with gradient magnitude in regression.
We therefore adopt activation patching [Meng et al., 2022a, Conmy et al., 2023], an intervention that
swaps activations between two forward passes: at each (modality, layer), we replace target-domain
2
activations with source-domain activations at modality-m token positions and measure the change in
predicted trajectory. We call the resulting score theCausal Importance Score(CIS); its values across
(m, l)form amodality–layer tensor. Measurements use fine-tuned VLM planners (Appendix B).
Applying CIS to three architecturally distinct VLMs (LFM2-VL-1.6B [Amini et al., 2025], Qwen2-
VL-2B [Wang et al., 2024], and PaliGemma2-3B [Beyer et al., 2024]) across two cross-city bench-
marks (nuScenes [Caesar et al., 2020] and Argoverse 2 [Wilson et al., 2023]) reveals two regular-
ities.(F1) Modality axis (Figure 1 (c)).Image-token shift dominates the earliest layers in every
VLM; text-token shift overtakes it in intermediate layers of LFM2-VL and Qwen2-VL, but never in
PaliGemma2. A single uniform alignment cannot target both modalities; this motivates per-modality
repair.(F2) Layer axis (Figure 1 (d)).Each VLM partitions into 2 or 3 causal zones ( Φalign, Φtune,
Φfreeze) with architecture-specific boundaries; within each architecture, the partition is identical
(LFM2-VL, PaliGemma2) or differs by at most one layer (Qwen2-VL) on nuScenes and Argoverse 2,
making the structure architecture-dependent and dataset-stable. This motivates architecture-adaptive
repair.
These findings recast cross-city domain shift as a two-dimensional tensor over modality and layer,
naturally motivating alocate-then-repairframework,MoLaRx(Modality–LayerRepair). Specif-
ically, (i)CIS locatesthe (modality, layer) sources of the cross-city gap and partitions layers into
functional zones; (ii)CIS-Guided Selective Repair (CISR) repairseach zone with a matched
operator: distribution alignment (MMD) on Φalign, parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA) on Φtune,
and freezing on Φfreeze. The operator composition is determined by the discovered partition, adapting
automatically to the underlying architecture. The contributions are summarized as follows:
• Discovery.We uncover a modality–layer structure of cross-city domain shift: image and text exhibit
distinct layer-wise patterns, and the zone partition is architecture-dependent and dataset-stable. To
our knowledge, this is the first joint modality–layer characterisation of cross-city shift in VLM
trajectory planners.
• Tool.We introduce CIS, an activation-patching diagnostic that measures how source-to-target
interventions at each (modality, layer) slice causally affect predicted trajectories.
• Method.We proposeMoLaRx, a locate-then-repair framework: CIS locates the modality–layer
sources of domain shift, and CISR routes existing adaptation operators (MMD, LoRA, and freezing)
to the diagnosed zones, with the composition adapting automatically to each architecture.
• Experiments.On three architecturally distinct VLMs across two benchmarks, MoLaRx attains
the smallest cross-domain gap among adaptation methods in all six main-table settings while
using 50–60% fewer trainable parameters and achieving 8.4–10.9 ms lower inference latency. In
contrast, uniform MMD-only adaptation collapses in all six settings, highlighting the importance
of structure-guided repair.
2 Related Work
Cross-domain trajectory planning has been studied for end-to-end driving [Filos et al., 2020, Yasarla
et al., 2025]; our work draws on two treatment paradigms. (1)Uniform domain adaptation in
trajectory planningapplies one adaptation strategy uniformly across the model. Feature align-
ment via adversarial losses [Ganin et al., 2016], kernel MMD [Gretton et al., 2012], or correlation
alignment [Sun et al., 2016], parameter-efficient adapters [Hu et al., 2022], and test-time normalisa-
tion [Wang et al., 2021] are the dominant variants [Oza et al., 2023]. Trajectory-prediction work adds
auxiliary perception regularisation [Han et al., 2025], domain-invariant graph encoders (T-GNN [Xu
et al., 2022]), or self-training on pseudo-trajectories across geographies (STraj [Zhang et al., 2025]);
VLM-based planners (GPT-Driver [Mao et al., 2023], DriveVLM [Tian et al., 2024], LMDrive [Shao
et al., 2024], DriveLM [Sima et al., 2024], LanguageMPC [Sha et al., 2023]) are typically evaluated
on a single benchmark distribution, which motivates studying cross-city shift within multimodal
planning backbones [Naeinian et al., 2026]. (2)Locate-then-editidentifies a small set of components
causally responsible for a target behaviour and modifies them directly. ROME [Meng et al., 2022a]
edits a single causally identified MLP site, while MEMIT [Meng et al., 2022b] distributes knowledge
updates across multiple identified MLP layers. MMEdit [Cheng et al., 2023] extends this to multi-
modal visual-fact editing, and Surgical Fine-Tuning (Surgical FT) [Lee et al., 2022] adapts a manually
chosen layer band by shift type. Related approaches also explore selective treatment along other axes:
CaDeT [Pourkeshavarz et al., 2024] disentangles causal and spurious latent factors, CRiTIC [Ahmadi
et al., 2024] gates inter-agent attention using discovered causal relations, and GALA [Sahoo et al.,
3
2025] selects which layers to update at test time by gradient alignment. MoLaRx diagnoses cross-city
shift jointly by modality and layer through activation interventions, and matches different existing
repair operators to the diagnosed zones.

5.1 Experimental Setting
Datasets.We evaluate on two cross-city trajectory-planning benchmarks under transductive unsuper-
vised domain adaptation, using target-test inputs without their trajectory labels for adaptation. On
nuScenes[Caesar et al., 2020] we use Boston-Seaport (BS) as source and Singapore (SG) as target,
with a stratified split yielding ∼26.6k train, 3,569 source test, and 3,500 target test samples. On
Argoverse 2 Sensor (A V2)[Wilson et al., 2023] we use Pittsburgh (PIT) as source and Miami (MIA)
as target, extracting858train,284ID-test, and1,393OOD-test samples from57validation logs.
Models.We evaluate three VLMs spanning the backbone-operator and decoder-attention axes.
LFM2-VL-1.6B[Amini et al., 2025] is a 16-layer hybrid backbone with a causal decoder;Qwen2-VL-
2B[Wang et al., 2024] is a 28-layer pure-Transformer with a causal decoder;PaliGemma2-3B[Beyer
et al., 2024] pairs a SigLIP encoder with a 26-layer Gemma2 prefix-LM decoder using bidirectional
image attention.
Evaluation Metrics.We report four metrics:ADE( ↓, m): mean per-waypoint ℓ2 error;FDE( ↓, m):
error at the final waypoint; ∆ADE (↓, m): cross-domain gap ADE(St)−ADE(S s); andPE( ↓, ms):
inference latency. The numbers are averaged over five random seeds.
Baselines.We compare against nine baselines in four categories: (1)no-adaptation reference:Source-
only, the fine-tuned planner with no cross-domain repair, establishing the gap floor against all DA
methods; (2)uniform / scalar-view DA, four representatives of the “align-everything” view we argue
against:Full LoRA[Hu et al., 2022] (parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) only, no DA signal),

2016] (adversarial domain-invariant alignment), andMMD-only[Gretton et al., 2012] (alignment
without task fine-tuning); (3)layer-selective, non-causal DA(LLRD[Howard and Ruder, 2018];
Freeze-Last-K[Kumar et al., 2022]), choosing layers via correlational or heuristic criteria; and (4)

Effectiveness Analysis.We summarize four key observations. 1)MoLaRxis the best-performing
adaptation method on all cells, outperforming the strongest non-MoLaRx baseline by 0.015–0.138 m
on quality metrics (ADE, FDE, ∆ADE), with margins exceeding the 2σ significance threshold on most

family (ROME⋆, Surgical FT) outperforms uniform/scalar-view DA on most cells, confirming that

structured shift: MMD-only doubles ADE (LFM2/nuScenes: 4.275→9.657 m), and even Full
LoRA + MMD is worse than Full LoRA alone on 17 of 18 quality cells by 0.01–0.09 m — “align-

6 Conclusion and Limitations
We uncover a modality–layer structure of cross-city domain shift in VLM trajectory planners: image
and text exhibit distinct layer-wise patterns, and the zone partition is architecture-dependent and
dataset-stable. To expose this structure, we introduce CIS, an activation-patching diagnostic that
measures how source-to-target interventions at each (modality, layer) slice causally affect predicted
trajectories. Guided by CIS,MoLaRxapplies a zone-matched operator composition (MMD /
LoRA / freezing) that adapts automatically to each architecture. On three architecturally distinct
VLMs across two cross-city benchmarks, MoLaRx attains the lowest cross-city trajectory-error gap
among adaptation methods in all six main-table settings, while training 50–60% fewer parameters
and running 8.4–10.9 ms faster. In contrast, uniform MMD-only adaptation collapses in all six
settings, highlighting the importance of structure-guided repair. The modality–layer tensor view
is task-agnostic in principle; empirically, we validate it only for cross-city trajectory planning on
decoder-only and prefix-LM VLMs, while extensions to other tasks and shift types remain untested.
Limitations and future work. (i) Hard-threshold partition.The elbow-based partition can
produce one or two boundary-layer flips on tightly-shifted benchmarks; future improvements include
continuous operator weighting and a shift-magnitude-aware threshold that scales with measured cross-
domain feature distance.(ii) Architecture and task scope.Generalisation to encoder–decoder VLMs
(BLIP-2, PaLI), cross-attention fusion architectures, and non-driving tasks (captioning, VQA, dense
prediction) with non-geographic shifts (weather, sensor, season) remains untested.(iii) Evaluation
coverage.Our main evaluation focuses on nominal driving conditions and displacement metrics.
Evaluation using official planning-oriented metrics remains future work. The preliminary long-
tail probe shows high trajectory errors across methods, leaving robustness to long-tail scenarios
unestablished.(iv) Method assumptions.CIS requires scene-matched source/target pairs and its
estimates depend on pairing quality; the pairs are drawn predominantly from prevalent scenes, so
shift components visible only in rare scenes may be missed, and estimating CIS on long-tail-stratified
pairs is a natural extension. The three-zone structure is empirical with bootstrap stability but no
theoretical universality guarantee.

A Implementation Details
A.1 VLM Selection
Our three VLMs are not an arbitrary convenience sample. The 2024 driving-VLM landscape
varies along two orthogonal axes, and an architectural generalisation claim must factor against
both:(H1) backbone operator, LTC-hybrid (alternating convolution + attention, e.g. LFM2-VL) vs.
pure-Transformer (e.g. Qwen2-VL); and(H2) decoder attention connectivity, causal-only (LFM2-VL,
Qwen2-VL) vs. prefix-LM with bidirectional image attention (PaliGemma2). The three VLMs
instantiate three corners of this 2×2 grid, so comparing LFM2 vs. Qwen2 varies only H1 at fixed
H2, and comparing Qwen2 vs. PaliGemma2 varies only H2 at fixed H1. Our main architecture-level
claim (§5.3) is that the zone count of the modality–layer decomposition is predicted by H2 (causal →
3 zones, prefix-LM → 2 zones) rather than H1, so both axes are required for the claim to be testable.

A.4 Baseline Description
We compare against nine baselines organised into four categories. All adapted baselines (categories
1–3) share MoLaRx’s input format, backbone, and base LoRA rank (r=16, α=32); they differ only
in the design choice each one targets, so the gap to MoLaRx isolates that single choice.
(1) No-adaptation reference.
•Source-only. The fine-tuned planner with no cross-domain repair.
(2) Uniform / scalar-view DA.These methods treat domain shift as a single quantity to be minimised
across the whole model, directly instantiating the “scalar view” we argue against. Source-only, Full
LoRA, Full LoRA + MMD, and MMD-only together span a 2×2 grid over {LoRA-tune,no tune} ×
{align,no align} , isolating the marginal effect of each scalar-view design choice; DANN is added as
a representative of the adversarial-alignment alternative.
• Full LoRA[Hu et al., 2022]. Rank- 16 LoRA adapters attached toeverybackbone layer, optimised
only with the task loss (the “tune, no align” corner).
• Full LoRA + MMD[Hu et al., 2022, Gretton et al., 2012]. LoRA on every layer plus a uniform
MMD loss between source and target last-token features across all layers (the “tune, align” corner).
• DANN[Ganin et al., 2016]. Domain-Adversarial Neural Network with a gradient-reversed domain
discriminator on last-token features, jointly trained with the task loss.
• MMD-only[Gretton et al., 2012]. Uniform MMD alignment on every layer,withoutany LoRA
adapters or task retraining (the “no tune, align” corner).
(3) Layer-selective, non-causal DA.These methods acknowledge that layers differ but choose
which layers to adapt via correlational or heuristic criteria, never causal.

• LLRD (Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay)[Howard and Ruder, 2018]. Depth-decaying learning
rateη l =η 0 ·γ L−l withγ=0.9, so deeper layers update less.
• Freeze-Last-K[Kumar et al., 2022]. Freezes the deepest K layers (K=|Φ freeze| per VLM: 6 for

B Visual-Grounding Fine-Tuning
Before applying CIS to any (VLM, dataset) cell, we fine-tune the planner with a recipe that jointly (i)
removes numerical text features from the prompt and (ii) supplies three consecutive front-camera
frames as visual context. The two interventions together force the planner to extract trajectory-relevant
kinematics (position, velocity, acceleration) from the image channel rather than from text shortcuts;
this is a prerequisite for the image-channel diagnostic in §4.1.
Modern VLMs trained on multimodal driving tasks frequently collapse onto atextual short-
cut[Geirhos et al., 2020]: whenever numerical text features (ego speed, following distance, lead
velocity) are sufficient statistics for the trajectory label, stochastic gradient descent drives the planner
to ignore the image channel. On a vanilla source-fine-tuned planner we measure ImgDep =−0.6%
(Eq. 22, Appendix A.3) — replacing all camera frames with a constant grey image leaves the tra-
jectory error essentially unchanged. In this regime the image row of the modality–layer tensor
Ψ is structurally zero, so any image-side causal claim from CIS would be vacuous; restoring a
non-degenerateImgDepis therefore a hard prerequisite, not a tuning detail.
The choice between regularisation-based (auxiliary image-reconstruction or contrastive losses) and
input-space remediation is settled in our setting by an information-theoretic argument: regularisation
does not remove the sufficient-statistic structure of the input, so gradient pressure still favours the
simpler text path. We instead modify the input space jointly, in two coupled steps.(1)Removing
numerical textmakes the text channelinsufficient: the conditional mutual information I(Y ∗;c task |
image) falls to near zero, so no purely-textual policy can drive the loss below a hard floor.(2)Adding
three temporal framesmakes the visual channelsufficient: position, velocity, and acceleration are
linearly recoverable from {It−1,I t,I t+1} at ∆t=0.5 s via finite differencing, soI(Y ∗;{I} t−1:t+1)
is large.2 (3)Joint effect: the only gradient-bearing path from input to target now runs through the
visual encoder. SGD does notchooseto use images — it has no other channel that can lower the loss.
Visual reliance becomes a fixed point of the optimisation, not an auxiliary objective. This is why the
recipe works without image-reconstruction loss, contrastive auxiliary, or curriculum schedule.

projected on the camera image. Together with Fig. 4, the qualitative case study confirms three distinct

explain the quantitative results in Sec. 5.

C.2.3 Temporal Frames Sensitivity (N frame)
We sweep Nframe ∈ {1,2,3,4,5} on each of the three VLMs on nuScenes (BS →SG), holding all
other CISR hyperparameters at their defaults. For every Nframe value we re-run the full pipeline
and report (i) cross-city ∆ADE per VLM, (ii) ImgDep (visual-dependence diagnostic, App. B) on
LFM2-VL as the canonical signal-collapse indicator, and (iii) the per-step GPU memory and inference
latency on a single A10 (LFM2-VL backbone; the cost trend is architecture-invariant since Nframe
only scales the visual-token sequence length).
As shown in Table 8,(i) Nframe=1 triggers the textual shortcut.ImgDep collapses to −0.6%,
indicating the planner has degenerated into producing trajectories from text-only conditioning; ∆ADE
rises by ∼1.0 m across all three VLMs over the Nframe=3 optimum.(ii) Nframe=2 recovers velocity
but not acceleration.First-order finite differencing yields velocity, but acceleration requires three
samples; ∆ADE improves over Nframe=1 by ∼0.5 m per VLM but still trails the optimum by∼0.5 m,
the residual gap attributable to missing second-order motion.(iii) Nframe=3 is the elbow on every
VLM.All three architectures bottom out at Nframe=3; the additional gain from Nframe=4 or Nframe=5
is ≤0.03 m on every VLM, well within seed noise.(iv) Nframe≥4 doubles cost for negligible gain.
Memory rises from 8.1 GB at Nframe=3 to 16.3 GB at Nframe=5 (2.0×), and latency from 63 ms
to 98 ms (1.6×), while ImgDep gains ≤1.3 pp and ∆ADE improves ≤0.03 m on every VLM.(v)
Architecture-shared optimum.Despite different absolute floors, all three VLMs share the same
elbow at Nframe=3, supporting a single architecture-agnostic default. In conclusion, Nframe=3 is
the minimum sufficient temporal context: it enables second-order motion (acceleration) via finite
differencing, yields the elbow ∆ADE on every VLM, and pays ∼50% of the memory and ∼65% of
the latency of Nframe=5. We use Nframe=3 in all main-paper experiments without per-VLM tuning,
consistent with the kinematic argument in App. B.

C.6 Reverse-Direction Transfer
The main paper evaluates one transfer direction per benchmark (BS→SG, PIT→MIA). Since transfer
can be asymmetric, we repeat the pipeline with source and target reversed. The reverse runs reuse the
forward code path, hyperparameters, and evaluation protocol: the source planner is retrained on the
reversed source city (for A V2, the planner is initialised from the nuScenes-trained visual-grounding
checkpoint as in the forward protocol), CIS is re-estimated, the repair strategies are retrained, and
evaluation uses fixed 300-sample ID and OOD subsets of the reversed split. These are diagnostic
runs: single seed, with the visual-grounding stage truncated from three to approximately two epochs;
the reversed source pools are also ∼4.7× smaller than the forward ones, so absolute errors are
comparable only within a direction. A V2 values come from a separate evaluation harness and are
comparable only within their row.

D Method Details
This section gives the technical details deferred from §4.2: the multi-scale MMD applied on Φalign
(§D.1), the LoRA factorisation applied onΦtune (§D.2), the architecture-adaptive operator composition
that handles the prefix-LM regime (§D.3), and the full procedure consolidated as Algorithm 1 together
with the rationale for the zone-matched design (§D.4).
D.1 MMD onΦ align
The MMD operator’s role is to align the marginal distributions ofimage-token representations
between source and target domains at layers where CIS identifies the domain shift as image-channel-
dominated. We adopt MMD [Gretton et al., 2012] rather than adversarial alignment (DANN [Ganin
et al., 2016], CDAN [Long et al., 2018]) or correlation matching (CORAL [Sun et al., 2016]) for three
reasons: (i) MMD is a closed-form, gradient-tractable distance between two empirical distributions
in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), so no auxiliary discriminator network or min-max
optimisation is required; (ii) the multi-scale RBF variant is consistent (zero iff distributions match)
without bandwidth tuning; (iii) MMD applies on raw representations, so it composes cleanly with the
per-zone operator routing in §D.3.

D.2 LoRA onΦ tune
The LoRA operator’s role is to apply a parameter-efficient correction to the layers where CIS identifies
the domain shift as text-channel-dominated. We adopt LoRA [Hu et al., 2022] over full fine-tuning to
(i) preserve the source-fine-tuned baseline as the initialisation by writing the update as a perturbation
BA that starts at zero, (ii) limit trainable parameters to r(din +d out) per projection so the small
target sample budget does not overfit, and (iii) compose cleanly with MMD onΦalign and freeze on
Φfreeze via per-zone gradient masking (Eq. 31). LoRA is the de facto PEFT default for fine-tuning
large language and multimodal models; selectively attaching it to Φtune rather than all layers (the Full
LoRA baseline) is what differentiates CISR from generic PEFT.
Factorisation.For each layer l∈Φ tune we attach LoRA adapters to every linear projection within
the layer—attention projections {Wq,W k,W v,W o} and feed-forward / convolution projections
{W1,W 2,W 3,W in}. For each such projection W0 ∈R dout×din, LoRA parameterises the post-fine-
tune weight as a rank-radditive update,
W=W 0 + α

where W0 is the frozen base-weight matrix, {A,B} are the trainable low-rank factors, r=16 is
the LoRA rank (the inner dimension of the factorisation), α=32 is the LoRA scaling factor (which
decouples the magnitude of the update from the rank), anddin, dout are the input and output dimensions
of the projection.
We follow the standard LoRA initialisation: A∼ N(0, σ 2

source-fine-tuned baseline; training departs from this baseline only in the directions where the target

r(din +d out)per projection (vs. the fulld in ·d out for full fine-tuning).
The domain shift on Φtune acts along a low-dimensional subspace—the projection of hidden states
onto numerical-magnitude directions (ego speed, following distance, lead-vehicle velocity) where
source and target distributions differ—rather than as a full-rank drift. A rank- r correction with r≪d
therefore captures the relevant component of the shift while preserving the source-side computation.

This empirical low-rank structure matches the rationale behind LoRA in language models [Hu et al.,
2022]: the intrinsic dimension of task-specific adaptation is small, so a rank-r correction with r≪d
suffices.
D.3 Adaptive operator composition
The role of the adaptive composition is to wire the three zone-specific operators (MMD on Φalign,
LoRA on Φtune, freeze on Φfreeze) into a single training objective, with gradients routed by zone, and
to handle the regime where one of the zones is empty (the prefix-LM case,Φtune=∅ on PaliGemma2).
The closest baseline in spirit is surgical fine-tuning [Lee et al., 2022], which selectively unfreezes
layers based on a manual shift-type heuristic (input-shift → early, feature-shift → middle, output-
shift → late). Our composition differs in two ways: (i) zone selection is data-driven via CIS rather
than heuristic, and (ii) the operator type per zone (MMD vs LoRA vs freeze) is matched to the type
of shift CIS measures, rather than uniformly applying LoRA-then-freeze.

D.4 Full procedure and zone-matched rationale
The three operators are matched to the type of shift that CIS measures in each zone, and each of the
three matches is necessary:(i) MMD on Φalign aligns two visual-appearance distributions via a rank-r
LoRA on attention projections: source and target features become statistically indistinguishable on
these layers, while the MLP-level feature transformation is preserved (no MLP adapter attached).
Since image-channel shift is concentrated here and is high-dimensional, distributional alignment is
the right tool.(ii) LoRA on Φtune (when this zone is non-empty) adds a low-rank correction that is
sufficient for the numerical-distribution shift but small enough to avoid overfitting to the source. Since
text-channel shift acts along a low-dimensional subspace, a parameter-efficient update is sufficient.
(iii) Freeze on Φfreeze preserves the task-critical invariant computation that cannot be improved by
adaptation. Updating these layers (e.g., via LLRD [Howard and Ruder, 2018] or full LoRA) only
29
adds noise and overfitting risk; freezing them is strictly better. The assignment is architecture-aware
by construction: the 3-operator instantiation emerges on causal decoders where all three zones are
non-empty, and the 2-operator instantiation (MMD + freeze) emerges on prefix-LM decoders where
the text-dominated zone collapses (Eq. 33). Applying any single operator uniformly across all layers
is strictly worse than this matched assignment; in the extreme case of global MMD (MMD-only
ablation), the result is catastrophic (§5.4).
