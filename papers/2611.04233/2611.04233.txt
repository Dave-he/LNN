Abstract.Foot ulcers are a leading cause of lower limb amputation,
and the highest burden often falls on low and middle income regions
where specialists and computing hardware are both scarce. Wound mon-
itoring tools are most useful there when they are accurate yet small
enough to run on ordinary devices, but accuracy usually comes from
heavy networks. We present LINDA, a compact model that grounds seg-
mentation in an explicit structural prior instead of raw intensity. A Local
Self-Similarity (LSS) map, computed directly from the image and stable
under local lighting changes, is fused into a ResNet-34 encoder, and a
small Liquid Time-Constant module refines the boundary over four iter-
ative steps. With every model trained under one protocol on the FUSeg
2021 dataset, LINDA obtains the best Dice (86.68%), IoU (79.67%), and
boundary accuracy (HD95 6.37 pixels) in our comparison while using
only25.70Mparameters,fewerthantheU-Netandtransformerbaselines.
As the LSS map is a fixed function of the input, it exposes the structural
cues the model follows in a readable form. Together this makes LINDA
an accurate, inspectable, and lightweight option for wound assessment
in resource-constrained care.

1 Introduction
Foot ulcers are a primary cause of non-traumatic lower-limb amputation [1],
and precise delineation of the wound bed is needed to track healing and plan
treatment [2]. Manual planimetry, the clinical gold standard, is subjective, slow,
and varies between observers, so automated segmentation has become a priority.

In many settings, and especially in low and middle income regions, this must
run with limited specialist time and limited computing hardware, which favors
models that are accurate yet small enough for modest devices.
Despite recent advances [3], automated foot ulcer segmentation remains a
hard problem. Ulcers vary widely in scale, lighting, and texture. The transition
zones between necrotic tissue, granulation tissue, and healthy skin are often am-
biguous, with low contrast and diffuse gradients [4]. Widely used architectures
such as U-Net variants [5] and Vision Transformers (ViTs) [6] perform well on
many medical segmentation tasks, yet wound boundaries can remain imprecise
when ulcer shapes are irregular and contrast with healthy skin is low. Convo-
lutional encoders have a limited receptive field, while the patch tokenization in
ViTs can blur fine boundary detail and lead to oversmoothed masks [7]. Most
of these models also predict the mask in a single forward pass, unlike the way a
human reader traces a boundary, revisiting and adjusting it as local and global
cues are weighed.
To address these limitations, we proposeLINDA, a boundary aware archi-
tecture for foot ulcer segmentation. First, a Local Self-Similarity (LSS) map is
computed directly from the input image and fused into a pretrained ResNet-
34 backbone, giving the early layers explicit local texture awareness. Second, a
Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) network [8] at the bottleneck acts as an iterative
refinement loop that resolves boundary ambiguities. Finally, the structural prior
stays visible: because the LSS map is computed before encoding and used as the
target of the boundary loss, it offers a concrete, readable view of the cues the
network follows, in the spirit of explainable AI in clinical imaging [9–11].
Our contributions are as follows. (1) An additive LSS fusion module injects
local patch-correlation statistics into the early encoder features, anchoring the
network to tissue boundaries without disturbing the pretrained weights. (2) An
LTC-driven refinement loop at the bottleneck uses iterative dynamics to refine
a global spatial token that conditions the decoder. (3) A Boundary Alignment
Loss ties the prediction to the LSS map, giving the lowest boundary error in
our comparison together with strong overlap accuracy at a low parameter count.
Overall, these components form a compact, interpretable foot ulcer segmenta-
tion model that matches the strongest baselines on region overlap and clearly
improves boundary precision, while using far fewer parameters, a good fit for
resource-constrained clinical settings.
2 Previous Work
Automated wound segmentation is built mostly on the U-Net family [5] and,
more recently, on transformer encoders [6,7]. Several methods target wounds
directly. Wang et al. [12] trained fully convolutional networks for wound re-
gion extraction, and WSNet [13] added a wound-oriented pipeline with dedi-
cated pre-processing. On the FUSeg 2021 benchmark, FUSegNet [14] reported
the strongest published result and remains the reference point for this dataset.
State-space (Mamba) models have also entered the field, from lightweight ulcer

Local self-similarity was introduced by Shechtman and Irani [20] to match
patterns across images and videos through internal patch correlations rather
than raw intensity. We reuse this idea as a deterministic prior and fold it into
the encoder. For interpretability, post-hoc attribution such as Grad-CAM and
SHAP is widely used in medical imaging [11,21], but these maps are produced
after training and can be unstable. Our LSS maps are fixed functions of the input
and serve as the target of the boundary loss, so they give a stable, readable view
of the structural evidence the model is trained to follow.
3 Methodology
LINDA is an end-to-end, boundary-aware segmentation network for clinical
wound images; Figure 1 gives an overview. It has four modules: a ResNet-34
encoder, an additive LSS fusion block, a Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) refine-
ment loop, and a deep-supervision decoder. Different modules are discussed in
the following subsections.

3.4 Interpretable Structural Priors
Because the LSS extractor is deterministic, its three channels carry a fixed, read-
able meaning rather than the shifting saliency of post-hoc attribution [21].LSS
Mean (µ)measures local homogeneity and highlights uniform regions such as
granulation tissue in the wound bed.LSS Maxkeeps the strongest local correla-
tion in each neighborhood, helping thin structures survive downsampling.LSS
Std (σ)responds to high local variance and marks the sharp transition between
wound and healthy skin. These maps are not a claim about what individual
network weights compute. They are deterministic inputs to the encoder, and the
Mean channel is also the target of the boundary loss (Eq. 5), so this part of the
pipeline can be inspected directly.
3.5 Compute, Data and Inference Time
For both training and evaluation, we have used single NVIDIA A100 GPU with
40 GB VRAM. We have performed all the experiments on MICCAI Foot Ulcer
Segmentation (FUSeg) Challenge dataset [22], details of which are described in
sec 4.1. Our model takes roughly5hours to train and∼45ms per image, on
average, for inference.
4 Results
4.1 Dataset and Implementation Details
We use the MICCAI Foot Ulcer Segmentation (FUSeg) Challenge dataset [22],
which has 1,210 images (810 train, 200 validation, 200 test) from 889 patients
under varied lighting and backgrounds. The test labels are held private by the
organizers,soallmetricsarereportedontheofficialvalidationset.Sampleimages
are shown in Figure 2. Images and masks are processed at512×512, the grid
also used for prediction. LINDA is implemented in PyTorch 2.5.1 and trained
on an NVIDIA A100. For a fair comparison, every model uses the same setup:
100 epochs, batch size 2, the Adam optimizer, and the checkpoint with the best
validation Dice.
The ResNet-34 encoder uses ImageNet-pretrained weights, while the LSS Ex-
tractor and LTC loop are trained from scratch. The task is binary, wound against
background; the necrotic and granulation regions noted earlier describe the vi-
sual difficulty inside the wound, not separate classes. We report Dice (DSC),
IoU, and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95), all on the wound class.

4.3 Ablation Studies
Model Components:Table 2 reports the ablation on different components of
the proposed model. Adding LSS and LTC to the ResNet-34 baseline without the
Boundary Alignment Loss (BAL) drops Dice from 85.22% to 76.18%: pixel-wise
losses alone do not bring the structural prior and the refinement together. BAL
fixes this. With LSS and BAL the model reaches 86.41% Dice, and the LTC loop
takes the full model to 86.68% Dice and 79.67% IoU. Two further rows isolate
the rest: keeping only LSS with the decoder, without the pretrained encoder, col-
lapses to 46.09% Dice, so the encoder is essential, while the gap from LSS+BAL
to the full model is the LTC contribution. The full model adds only 0.83M pa-
rameters over the baseline, and this pattern reinforces the interpretability point:
the LSS prior is used well only once the boundary loss ties the prediction to it.
Number of refinement steps:Table 3 varies the number of LTC steps
T. Performance is stable forT=1toT=8, andT=4gives the best balance of
overlap (86.68% Dice, 79.67% IoU) and boundary accuracy (HD95 6.37) at a low
step count. BeyondT=8it collapses, with Dice below 73% and HD95 above 22
pixels, consistent with the Euler-discretized ODE becoming unstable over too
many steps. We therefore useT=4.

5 Discussion
We presentedLINDA, a foot ulcer segmentation model that fuses a determinis-
tic Local Self-Similarity prior into a ResNet-34 encoder and refines the prediction
with a small Liquid Time-Constant module and a boundary alignment loss. It
produces accurate masks with the tightest boundaries in our comparison at only
25.70M parameters, and since the LSS maps are computed before encoding,
they also give a readable view of the cues the model follows, with no separate
explanation step, thus making it a faithful and explainable segmentation model.

6 Impact in RCS
In resource constrained setting, it is crucial to balance performance, model size
and inference speed. Our proposed approach, achieves the best tradeoff between
model size (25.70M parameters), training time (∼5hrs) and per-image inference
time(∼45ms).LINDAachievesstate-of-the-artscoresinDiceandHD95metrics
with a reasonably low number of parameters and GFLOPS, as compared to its
closest competitors, thus making it an excellent choice for a wound segmentation
model in a resource constrained setting.
